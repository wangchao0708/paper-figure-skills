\section*{NeurIPS figure layout examples}

These examples use the official NeurIPS 2026 single-column template. The
top row contains two panels that each occupy 48\% of the text width, placed
side by side. The bottom row contains one full-width panel. All three
panels were generated by \texttt{figspec} at their final physical size and
are included without a width argument.

\noindent
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\textwidth}
\centering
\includegraphics{showcase-neurips-single-a.pdf}
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\textwidth}
\centering
\includegraphics{showcase-neurips-single-b.pdf}
\end{minipage}

\medskip
\textbf{Single-column layout.} The two panels are 190.8\,pt (2.64\,in)
wide each, or 48\% of the 397.5\,pt text block. Their labels and legends
are 8\,pt, ticks 7\,pt, and panel titles 9\,pt. Because each PDF is already
at the final size, the minipages only position the panels; they do not
scale them. The left panel uses a blue/orange palette, and the right panel
uses a green/purple palette so that the two panels remain visually distinct
while sharing the same typographic hierarchy. The draw.io equivalents are
253.9\,px for each panel, with label/legend sizes of 10.6, tick sizes of
9.3, and title sizes of 12.0 units.

\vspace{1em}
\begin{center}
\includegraphics{showcase-neurips-double.pdf}
\end{center}

\textbf{Double-column layout.} The lower panel is 397.5\,pt (5.50\,in)
wide and fills the text block. It uses a third, red/teal palette. This is
the same figure width that \texttt{figspec spec NeurIPS double} reports;
the labels, ticks, and title keep the same sizes as the single-column
panels. The draw.io equivalent is 529.1\,px wide. The surrounding text is
set in the NeurIPS template so that the relationship between the
template's type scale and the figures is visible without relying on a
screenshot.

\vspace{1em}
Both rows are shown at natural size on a page cropped to the content. In a
paper, the single-column panels can be treated as a two-panel figure, while
the double-column panel is a separate full-width figure.
